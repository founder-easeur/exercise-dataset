# Exercises for wrist, hand and finger problems | NHS inform

Source: https://www.nhsinform.scot/illnesses-and-conditions/muscle-bone-and-joints/arm-shoulder-and-hand-problems-and-conditions/exercises-for-wrist-hand-and-finger-problems

Learn exercises to help with problems with joints in the hands and fingers.

This information may be useful for those who have a new wrist, hand or finger problem. People who have been diagnosed with a wrist, hand or finger condition or have had recent surgery in the area should follow the advice of their healthcare professional.

After any problem in the wrists, hands or fingers, it's important to get movement and strength back. This supports tissue healing and will help you get moving again.

When doing exercise you should listen to your pain levels, especially in the early stages. You may find that these exercises increase your symptoms slightly in the beginning. However, they should get easier over time and, with regular practice, can help to improve movement in the wrists, hands or fingers.

If you start to feel unwell stop these exercises immediately.

## Full finger bend

Slowly bend your fingers into a full fist and then straighten them again. Repeat this movement, aiming for a comfortable range of motion.

## Spreading and closing finger

With your hand resting on a table, spread your fingers apart as far as is comfortable, then close them together again. Repeat smoothly.

## Tip of finger bend

Bend the tips of your fingers down while keeping the rest of the finger straight, then straighten. Repeat for each finger.

## Finger duck and hook

Start with your fingers straight, then bend only the middle joints so the fingertips duck toward the palm, forming a hook. Return to straight. Repeat.

## Flat fist

Bend your fingers at the large knuckles while keeping the middle and tip joints straight, forming a flat tabletop with your fingers. Return to straight. Repeat.

## Palm up/palm down

Rest your forearm on a table with your elbow bent. Rotate your forearm so your palm faces up, then rotate so it faces down. Repeat smoothly.

## Mid finger bend

Bend the middle joints of your fingers while keeping the knuckles and fingertips straight. Return and repeat.

## Thumb stretch

Gently pull your thumb away from your fingers and hold for 20 to 30 seconds, feeling a stretch at the base of the thumb.

## Tip of thumb bend

Bend the tip of your thumb across your palm while keeping the base of the thumb still, then release. Repeat.

## Thumb and finger touch

Touch the tip of your thumb to the tip of each finger in turn, forming an O shape, then open the hand again. Repeat for each finger.

## Wrist bend

Support your forearm and gently bend your wrist forward and backward through a comfortable range. Repeat smoothly.

## Hand wave

Support your forearm with the thumb facing up and move the wrist up and down through its full range of motion, as if waving.

## How many and how often

### Movement and strengthening exercises

When starting new exercises, it can be helpful to do 2 to 3 repetitions at a time. It's better to do small amounts throughout the day. As this gets easier, add 1 or 2 repetitions to your movements every few days. Over time aim for a maximum of 2 sets of 15.

### Stretching exercises

Once you can comfortably perform a short hold, hold the position for a longer period of time as a stretch. You should try to hold stretches for 20 to 30 seconds if possible. Try to focus on doing sets of exercises. For example, do 2 to 3 sets of stretches. Aim to do this 2 to 3 times a day.
